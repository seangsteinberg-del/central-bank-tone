Methodology: how tone is scored, and how well it works

The value of this platform is a tone signal, so the signal is measured against labeled data rather than asserted. The FOMC-benchmark accuracy below is reproducible offline with make eval; the rate validation is computed live on the Signal vs Market page from this corpus.

Three scorers, cross-checked

Gemini LLM judge (production, multilingual) Supervised TF-IDF classifier (offline) Deterministic hawkish/dovish lexicon (audit floor)

Each speech gets a tone label and a continuous score in [-1, +1]. The independent scorers are a transparent cross-check on the model: when a majority disagree with the headline's direction, the speech is flagged flagged for review rather than the disagreement being averaged away.

The production pipeline: structured, not a single call

The state of the art (and our own benchmarking) is clear that a single whole-speech LLM call is the weakest method. The production tone is built in steps instead.

  1. Headline: the Gemini holistic whole-speech score. It keeps the dynamic range a macro reader needs to see moves and divergence, and it is the comparable index over time.
  2. Decompose: split into sentences, keep the policy-relevant ones, and classify each as hawkish / dovish / neutral with an aspect and a forward/backward horizon. Aggregating these gives a rate-path measure (forward-looking policy intent, separated from backward-looking description) and a per-aspect breakdown (inflation, growth, employment, balance sheet, and so on).
  3. Cross-check by direction: the structured sentence-level net, the supervised classifier (only for the Fed, where it is measured to be valid), and the lexicon each vote on the direction. The share that disagree with the headline is an explicit uncertainty band; a majority dissent flags the speech for review. The comparison is by direction, not magnitude, so the signals are never falsely flagged for being on different scales.

Does the production signal track Fed policy?

This measures the platform's own headline, on this corpus, against rates: a monthly Federal Reserve tone index from the stored scores vs FRED's fed funds rate and 2-year Treasury yield (2023-2026, 39 months, 366 Fed speeches), each with a family-wise, moving-block bootstrap CI (ADR 0023). These figures are computed live on the Signal vs Market page.

On the effective fed funds rate the index does not just move with the cycle (same-month +0.67); it leads it (+0.72 at three months), and the lead survives the stricter test: the family-wise, moving-block bootstrap interval is [+0.42, +0.85], still clear of zero after correcting for the overlapping windows and the twelve simultaneous tests (ADR 0023). Read it with care, though: fed funds is administered and telegraphed, so part of a "lead" is a hawkish regime simply staying hawkish. Against the freely-repricing 2-year yield, the cleaner test, the relationship is contemporaneous only (+0.27) and every interval includes zero, so we do not claim the tone leads the 2-year. This is in-sample correlation on the Fed alone, not out-of-sample PnL. See Signal vs Market for the rate-path index and the current divergence.

Does it work? Accuracy on the FOMC benchmark

Held-out test split of "Trillion Dollar Words" (Shah, Paturi, Chava, ACL 2023): FOMC sentences labeled hawkish / dovish / neutral. Every hyperparameter was chosen on train only.

The classifier's gain over the lexicon is statistically significant: McNemar p = 0.012, and the bootstrap 95% CI on the accuracy gap is [+2.2%, +14.1%] (excludes zero). It is a credible linear baseline, not a claim of state of the art.

Are the confidences trustworthy?

Accuracy alone hides whether a model is sure when it is wrong. Binning test predictions by confidence and comparing each bin's stated confidence to its observed hit-rate gives the calibration error.

Expected calibration error ECE = 0.142, maximum MCE = 0.276. The error is one-sided: the classifier is under-confident (mean confidence near 0.46 against 59.9% accuracy), so its probability is a conservative lower bound on how often it is right, not an over-statement to be discounted. That is the expected shape for a three-class softmax, which divides probability mass across the classes.

Does the tone track policy?

Annual FOMC tone vs the fed funds rate and the 2- and 10-year Treasury yields (FRED), 1996-2022.

Regressing the same-year change in the 2-year Treasury yield on annual tone gives a slope of +6.89 (bootstrap 95% CI [+2.31, +14.22], excludes zero; Pearson r = +0.35). In years the FOMC sounded more hawkish, the market repriced near-term policy higher within the same year. This is a descriptive, same-year link, not a predictive or causal claim; the next-year lead terms are near zero, as expected for an already-public signal.

Honest limitations

  • The supervised classifier is a linear bag-of-words model; fine-tuned transformers score higher.
  • It does not transfer well out of distribution: applied unchanged to op-fed (FOMC meeting transcripts, a different annotation method), it drops to 32% accuracy / 0.318 macro-F1, below that corpus's majority baseline. Much of its signal is specific to the speech corpus it was trained on (docs/research/cross-dataset-eval.md).
  • The economic link is annual and descriptive over a small sample, not a trading signal.
  • The production-signal rate validation is the Federal Reserve only, where free market ground truth exists; the other seven institutions are scored but their tone is not validated against rates here. The link is correlation over a single monthly cycle, not out-of-sample PnL.
  • Tone is compared only within a committee, not across banks: a bank's level reflects its own communication style and language, so a Fed +0.3 and a BoJ +0.3 are not the same reading.
  • The holistic headline is emitted on a coarse anchored scale with a large neutral mass, so it is best read as a direction and a trend, not a precise cardinal value; month-to-month moves below about 0.1 are within the model's granularity.
